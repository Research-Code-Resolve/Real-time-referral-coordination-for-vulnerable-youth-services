## PROBLEM 1: LACK OF REAL-TIME REFERRAL COORDINATION FOR VULNERABLE YOUTH SERVICES

# The Problem:
Social workers supporting vulnerable youth struggle to make timely referrals because there is no centralized system showing which organizations currently have available services. Referrals for youth with disabilities, those needing emergency shelter, or those who have aged out of programs often require multiple phone calls and personal contacts to find an organization that can accept them. This delays access to critical support and sometimes leaves youth without assistance.
# Who Is Affected:
Vulnerable youth in urban communities, including youth with disabilities, homeless youth, survivors of abuse, and young people who age out of support programs. Social workers and community organizations in Kampala who struggle to coordinate referrals efficiently.
# What Has Been Tried:
Referrals are mostly done through informal networks, phone calls, and personal contacts between social workers and organizations. Some organizations keep internal partner lists, but these are often outdated and do not show real-time service availability. Without a shared digital platform, social workers spend significant time searching for available services.
# Where Technology Could Help:
A simple digital referral coordination platform that allows social workers to view available services across organizations in real time. The system should include a searchable directory of organizations, types of services offered, eligibility criteria, and current capacity (shelter spaces, program slots). A mobile-friendly interface would allow organizations to update their availability and enable social workers to make faster, more accurate referrals.
